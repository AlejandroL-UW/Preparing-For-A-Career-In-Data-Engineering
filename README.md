## Purpose
As a UW Bothell Computing & Software Systems (CSS) or Applied Computing (AC) student, you may know that you want to work in data engineering but still be unsure how to prepare for that career. You might already have useful experience from previous jobs, classes, or projects without knowing how it connects to your goal. Preparing early helps you recognize which experiences relate to data engineering and where you still need to grow. **This plan covers reviewing your experience, preparing a data engineering-focused resume, and building connections with people in the field before graduation.**

## Reviewing Your Experience
### Understanding Why Your Experience Matters
Not every useful skill comes from a job with a software-related title. Technical troubleshooting, organizing information, explaining complex topics, and working with others can relate to data engineering. Reviewing previous experience helps you recognize skills that may transfer to a field centered on reliable data and technical problem-solving. It can also show where you need more practice before applying.

### Identifying Skills You Can Explain
Review your previous jobs, class assignments, and personal projects. Write down the tasks you actually completed and what you learned from them. Group your examples by technical skills, working with information, problem-solving, and teamwork. Compare them with a few entry-level data engineering job descriptions, especially their expectations around data, software, and collaboration. Keep examples you can explain instead of claiming tools you have not used. Those notes give you a starting point for your resume.

## Preparing Your Resume
### Understanding Why Resume Preparation Matters
A resume can contain plenty of experience but still hide the skills a data engineering employer wants to see. This becomes a bigger concern when your previous jobs were in different technical fields. UW Bothell Career Services provides resume resources and review appointments that can help you evaluate how your experience is presented [[1]](#ref-1), [[2]](#ref-2).

### Organizing What You Want Employers to See
Start by reviewing your current resume rather than writing a completely new one. Check that your current UW Bothell education is listed and that relevant technical experience is easy to find. Emphasize examples involving data, software, troubleshooting, or improving a system when they accurately describe your work. Remove repeated descriptions. Ask UW Bothell Career Services for feedback, then make changes based on that review [[2]](#ref-2). Keep a copy of the earlier version so you can compare your progress. The goal here is to prepare a resume for future data engineering applications, not explain every resume-writing technique.

## Building Professional Connections
### Understanding Why Relationships Matter
Connecting with data engineers, analysts, and other technical professionals can help you understand how people enter data-focused roles and what their day-to-day work involves. In an informational interview, Ed Draper described networking as “maintaining professional relationships with lots of people and not burning bridges when you leave a job” [[3]](#ref-3). He also explained that a former coworker might reach out years later about a position. That makes maintaining relationships important even when you are not looking for work yet.

### Staying Connected With People in the Field
Use LinkedIn or UW Husky Landing to find data engineers, UW alumni working with data, or people in related technical roles [[4]](#ref-4). Introduce yourself as a UW Bothell student interested in data engineering and ask one genuine question about their career path. When you have a conversation, thank the person afterward and make note of what you learned. ## Checking Your Progress
### Understanding Why Reviewing Progress Matters
As you learn what data engineering employers expect, you may discover gaps between your current experience and your career goal. Reviewing your progress helps you decide which preparation activity matters most right now.

### Revisiting Your Preparation
As graduation gets closer, review your experience notes, resume, and conversations with professionals in data-related roles. Identify one change you made and one data engineering requirement you still need to understand. Use resume feedback and those conversations to choose your next step. This keeps your plan realistic without presenting technical projects or courses as activities you are currently doing.
